A bus terminal for the next century.

The busiest bus terminal in the world is 75 years old. The plan replaces it, restores the George Washington Bridge, and rebuilds the ramps into the Lincoln Tunnel. It is the second-largest line in the budget.

Today

The world's busiest bus terminal, built in 1950

Every weekday morning the Exclusive Bus Lane flips one lane of Route 495 against traffic and runs more than 1,850 buses through the Lincoln Tunnel, saving each rider 15 to 20 minutes. They arrive at a terminal the agency calls “the poster child of legacy infrastructure.”

Replacing it has been argued over since 2013. The 2019 proposal drew what the agency describes as an extreme negative response; the community's ask was simple: get buses off the streets. The 2021 plan did that, and doubled the cost.

Why now

Approved, financed, and in the ground

Between 2021 and 2025 the project secured its environmental record of decision, a unanimous City Council vote, financing and a payment-in-lieu-of-taxes agreement. In 2025 construction began on the Dyer Avenue deck-overs, which will hold temporary bus operations and, in the end, a 3.5-acre park.

Alongside it, the last plan rebuilt the Goethals and Bayonne bridges, the first major bridge construction since 1931, and moved every crossing to cashless tolling, which the agency says cut crashes by 57 percent.

By 2035

Buses off the streets, a park on top

Phase one, by 2029 or 2030: new ramps straight into the Lincoln Tunnel with a bypass lane for stalled buses, and a storage and staging building for up to 350 buses that doubles as the temporary terminal. Phase two, by 2035: a 2.1 million square foot main hall, street-facing shops, and the park.

It is designed for net-zero emissions and an all-electric bus fleet. The project page estimates 6,000 union construction jobs.
