Today

Four minutes under the Hudson

Tap a phone at Grove Street, ride two stops, lose your signal under the river, surface in the brightest room in the city. Newark to the World Trade Center takes 22 minutes and runs around the clock. In 2023 the system carried 50.5 million trips.

The last plan spent $3 billion making it reliable: a $1 billion signal system, a fleet 20 percent larger, nine-car trains on the Newark line, a rebuilt Harrison station, tap-to-pay and countdown clocks. The infrastructure is ready for more trains. The plan runs them.

Why now

PATH Forward finishes in early 2026

PATH Forward, the $430 million program repairing stations, tracks and switches, closes out in early 2026. With it done, the agency can add service instead of patching. The headwind is money: fares cover about a quarter of each ride, ridership fell in the pandemic, and PATH got no federal COVID relief.

So the plan pairs service with a fare schedule: 25 cents in 2026, then 25 cents each January through 2029, and new fare gates to stop evasion.

By 2035

More trains when riders need them

In 2026, for the first time in 25 years, all four lines run seven days a week. Direct weekend service from Journal Square and Hoboken to 33rd Street and from Hoboken to the World Trade Center. Friday late-night service doubled to Saturday levels. More Newark–WTC trains in the rush hour from 2027.

Underneath it, all-new uptown tracks and the control upgrades to run them faster and more reliably.
